# Public Health Radar India and South-East Asia

Date: 2026-05-17

Note: This refresh incorporates the strongest current official signals I found published between 16 March 2026 and 14 May 2026. I did not find a newer material official release dated 16-17 May 2026 that changed the overall picture.

## What changed

1. WHO South-East Asia has shifted its regional surveillance stack from scattered updates to a more centralized operating model. The WHO Health Emergencies Programme launched a South-East Asia Public Health Intelligence dashboard covering influenza, COVID-19, mpox, dengue, and Nipah, replacing SEARO's previous COVID-only dashboard.
2. Dengue and mpox remain live regional watchpoints. In the WHO SEARO bulletin covering the period 23 February to 8 March 2026, India reported 3,019 dengue cases in February and 6,563 cumulative dengue cases by 28 February; Sri Lanka reported 5,724 February cases; Timor-Leste reported 2,105 and remained in a large outbreak. The same bulletin reported one new mpox case from India in weeks 9-10 and 1,206 cumulative laboratory-confirmed mpox cases with 15 deaths across the region since July 2022.
3. Myanmar remains the clearest humanitarian public-health stress case in the region. WHO's 9 March 2026 Public Health Situation Analysis, highlighted in the May 2026 SEARO bulletin, says conflict, disasters, and economic collapse are constraining access to basic services; around 1.5 million children under five have missed basic vaccinations since 2018; and disease outbreaks are rising amid water, sanitation, and service disruption.
4. WHO is also pushing the region toward a stronger One Health operating model. On 7 April 2026, WHO South-East Asia called for science-led action on climate-linked, environmental, and infectious-disease risks and noted that a South Asia hub of the Global Heat Health Information Network is being advanced under the WHO-WMO climate and health programme.
5. That policy direction is now showing up in workforce design. WHO said on 16 March 2026 that One Health field epidemiology competencies are being integrated into training in Bangladesh and India and are planned in Sri Lanka, with emphasis on surveillance, outbreak investigation, data analysis, and cross-sector coordination.
6. South-East Asia is adding more structure to outbreak research readiness, not just response operations. WHO SEARO and CEPI's April 2026 consultation in New Delhi brought together all ten member states around a six-pillar vaccine and immunization research preparedness framework, with stress on pre-approved protocols, stronger clinical-trial networks, genomic surveillance, rapid financing, and regulatory harmonization.
7. Relatedly, WHO's Unity Studies network is becoming a more practical evidence-generation mechanism across the region. As of 20 April 2026, six sites across Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Thailand were aligned around standardized protocols and integrating early investigation studies into national multi-source surveillance systems.
8. India's public-health reform agenda is broadening from immunization and outbreak control into frontline capability and chronic-disease management. On 8 May 2026, the Union Health Ministry launched integrated training for primary healthcare teams to strengthen last-mile delivery through a unified competency-driven model; on 3 May 2026, it issued a first structured national framework for screening, diagnosis, treatment, and long-term management of diabetes mellitus in children.
9. India is also improving the data and digital plumbing behind public-health execution. A state-level ICD-10 mortality coding training programme opened at AIIMS Nagpur on 8 May 2026 to strengthen cause-of-death data systems, while the National Health Authority's 9 May 2026 AB PM-JAY showcase pushed responsible AI, ABDM enablement, and scalable digital health infrastructure. Separately, U-WIN had registered 11.87 crore children and 3.96 crore pregnant women as of 18 March 2026.

## Why it matters

- The regional story is increasingly about operating-system capability rather than single programmes: surveillance dashboards, outbreak analytics, research readiness, workforce design, mortality coding, and digital health rails.
- India's public-health agenda is widening in two directions at once: stronger last-mile frontline execution and broader coverage of noncommunicable and specialized conditions such as childhood diabetes, alongside continued immunization and insurance-platform digitization.
- South-East Asia's near-term operational risks still cluster around dengue, mpox monitoring, climate-linked heat and vector pressure, zoonotic spillover preparedness, and conflict-disrupted service delivery in Myanmar.
- For Deloitte-style strategy work, the durable themes are digital public-health infrastructure, One Health capability, evidence generation during outbreaks, frontline workforce enablement, and resilience in fragile settings.

## Watch next

- Whether WHO SEARO's Public Health Intelligence dashboard becomes the main live regional source for dengue, mpox, Nipah, and respiratory-pathogen monitoring.
- Whether pre-monsoon dengue activity in India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Thailand, and Timor-Leste accelerates in later 2026 bulletins.
- Whether India's new primary-care training and childhood-diabetes guidance translate into state-level implementation milestones rather than remaining summit outputs.
- Whether India's U-WIN, ABDM, and AI-for-claims efforts start generating measurable improvements in follow-up, fraud control, and service delivery quality.
- Whether Myanmar's access constraints further worsen vaccine-preventable disease, cholera, and routine-care disruption.
